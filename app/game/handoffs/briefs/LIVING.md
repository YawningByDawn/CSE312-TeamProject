The owner: "Make the game feel way more lived in and fleshed out. It should be a real world you conquer with many professions, many people and events, daily life going about… Rain and weather appropriately too… people you influence in the mine should be able to go aboveground… ending a turn is like a fast forward of watching people do stuff in the day, and as they happen flavor text/notifications appear."

## Build (`src/sim/living/**` — deterministic, seeded, headless, pure functions of state + seed; no rendering)
1. **Population.** Generate the society from the seed: ~60 mine workers in ≥ 20 trades and ~400 townspeople in ≥ 40 trades (spec §3 lists; design more), as named individuals in the mine and the key town people, and as headcount groups for the rest. Each person: trade, household (who they live with), a home above ground (miners live in the rows over the pit), a work place, a shift, a tavern / chapel / market habit, 2–4 ties (kin, friend, debtor, rival, lover), one trait, temperament. Trades differ in rules terms: where they go, what they see, whom they meet, what they can do for the god (data table: e.g. hauler touches every cart; lamplighter walks every street at dusk; midwife is admitted everywhere; clerk sees the count).
2. **Daily plans.** For every person and group an ordered plan of the day as timed stops (`{ t0, t1, place, activity }`, t in 0–1 from dawn to dusk plus the evening and night): wake, eat, walk, lift down, work (by trade), break, lift up, market / tavern / chapel / wash-place / home, sleep — varying by calendar day, weather and events. This is what BEHAV stages and what the sim uses for "who is near whom" (reach of whispers, witnesses, seeds of colour at followers' homes and haunts above ground).
3. **Calendar.** A seven-day week (work days, payday, market day, the Flame's day, a rest day) that changes plans, crowds, the guards' rounds, the count and the crystal carts to the city.
4. **Weather.** A seeded weather model per day with changes within the day (clear, overcast, drizzle, rain, storm, fog, wind, cold dawn) as a timeline `{ t, kind, strength, windDir }`, with its rule effects as data (rain: people indoors, processions hidden, lower workings flood → less digging there; fog: watchers and searches see less; storm: the lift stops early; wind: rumours and bells carry).
5. **Events.** ≥ 60 world events with triggers (calendar, weather, state, chance), a place, the people involved, a `time` in the day, a plain one-line flavour key + slots, and small real effects (opportunities and closed doors: a grieving widow is easier to turn; a brawl draws guards from the lift; a festival fills the chapel and empties the rows); some are chains over several days; the god's own doings (acts of evil, searches, exposures) echo into them (a funeral after a devouring; a sermon after a purge). Output per day: an ordered `feed` of `{ time, kind, place, who[], textKey, slots, weight }` for the Day's notifications, merged with the sim's own events.
6. **Interface first** (top of `handoffs/LIVING.md` within your first ten tool calls): `createSociety(seed, layout)`, `planDay(society, dayIndex, simFacts) → { calendar, weather, plans, events, feed, effects }`, the id lists (trades, activities, places, event kinds, weather kinds), and exactly what you need from the sim (`simFacts`) and what you give back (`effects` the sim applies: modifiers to strike rolls by zone, to whisper odds by person, to search chances, to Suspicion). Additive only afterwards. The sim builder wires it in behind one call; until then prove it standalone.

## Proof
Unit tests: determinism; every person has a valid home / work / plan with no teleport (consecutive stops are reachable in the time given); trades and headcounts; calendar effects; weather sequences plausible over 60 days (no 20-day storms; rain on ~25–35 % of days); every event can fire and its effect applies and expires; chains complete; feed ordering and density (a normal day yields 8–16 notes, never 0, never 40). Print a readable **"week in the life"** for one seed into the handoff: per day the weather, the calendar day, the feed lines (fallback text), and three people's full plans. Self-grade per rule 13 (does the week read like a place where people live?); do not report below an honest 8; milestones that each compile and pass tests: M1 interface + population + plans; M2 calendar + weather + effects; M3 events + chains + feed; M4 the week print, tests, DECISIONS note.
